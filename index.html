    Give globally

    Fight hunger in Burkina Faso and Nigeria

    Conflict, displacement, and climate shocks have pushed millions of people in both countries into hunger. Every group below works in these countries and has been checked by independent charity evaluators.

    2.7 millionpeople in Burkina Faso facing acute food insecurity (2024), up from 700,000 in 2019
    Nearly 35 millionNigerians facing food insecurity, a record. 5.8 million in the northeast expected to face severe hunger in 2026

    Source: World Food Programme country pages for Burkina Faso and Nigeria.

    How we checked these groups

    • Independent ratings. Each one is a GiveWell top charity or holds a Charity Navigator rating of 3 or 4 stars.
    • Registered US charity. Each is a registered 501(c)(3), so your gift is tax-deductible and the group must file public financial reports with the IRS.
    • Proof money reaches people. Each one publishes monitoring data, audits, or coverage surveys, not just stories.
    • Works in the country. Each runs programs in Burkina Faso, Nigeria, or both, confirmed on its own site or by GiveWell.

    Give safely and avoid scams

    • Give on the charity's own website or through GiveWell. Type the address yourself instead of clicking links in texts, WhatsApp, or social media messages.
    • Never send money by gift card, wire transfer, or cash app to a person who says they're collecting for a charity.
    • Be careful with crowdfunding pages and personal appeals. They usually aren't audited, and you can't check where the money goes.
    • Look up any charity on the IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search and on Charity Navigator before you give.
    • No charity working in a conflict zone can promise that every dollar arrives. The groups above track their work closely, report problems in public, and are reviewed by outside evaluators.

    Ratings and figures last checked September 26, 2026. Ratings can change, so check the evaluator's page before a large gift.

    About Mr. ASA

    A student-run platform working on food security in Prince George's County.

    Our mission

    Food help in Prince George's County is spread across dozens of pantries, churches, schools, and county programs, each with its own hours and rules. Mr. ASA puts it in one place so anyone can find food quickly, and so people who want to help can find where they're needed.

    We started with University of Maryland students and grew to serve the whole county, with a focus on PGCPS schools and the families they serve.

    Get in touch

    To add or correct a listing, list volunteer needs, or match a food drive with a pantry, contact the Mr. ASA team. Include the organization name, what changed, and a link to the official source.

    FAQ

    Common questions

    Do I need to show ID or proof of income?

    It depends on the site. Many pantries welcome walk-ins with no paperwork. Some ask for proof of county residency, and senior meal sites are for adults 60 and older. Each listing shows its rules.

    What does TEFAP mean on a listing?

    TEFAP is the federal Emergency Food Assistance Program. Sites that hand out TEFAP food may ask you to confirm your address and household income on a short form.

    Can volunteering count toward PGCPS service hours?

    Many opportunities do. Check the service hours column and confirm with your school's service-learning coordinator before you start.

    Can UMD students use the county sites too?

    Yes, if they meet the site's rules. Many sites are open to anyone. The UMD Students page also lists campus-only resources.

    Need help now?Call or text 2-1-1 (Maryland 211), available 24/7, for food, housing, and utility help.
